What is MiCA authorization and why does this list matter?

The Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) is the EU's comprehensive framework for crypto-asset markets. It took full effect on December 30, 2024, creating a single EU-wide licensing regime for crypto-asset service providers. Before MiCA, CASPs operated under a patchwork of national registrations -- a German BaFin license did not entitle you to operate in France, and a Lithuanian registration carried different requirements than an Irish one.

MiCA changes this. A CASP authorized by any national competent authority (NCA) in the EU receives a single passport to provide its authorized services across all 27 EU member states plus the three EEA countries (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein). Authorization requires meeting uniform capital requirements, governance standards, conduct-of-business rules, and -- critically for compliance teams -- anti-money laundering obligations under the EU's Transfer of Funds Regulation (TFR, Regulation (EU) 2023/1113), which implements the crypto Travel Rule.

This register is the definitive answer to "who is authorized?" When evaluating a counterparty, onboarding a new exchange partner, or assessing whether a CASP can legally operate in your jurisdiction -- this is the primary source. If a CASP claims MiCA authorization but does not appear in the ESMA register, treat that claim with extreme caution.

What services can MiCA-authorized CASPs provide?

MiCA Article 3(1)(16) defines ten categories of crypto-asset services. Each CASP is authorized for specific services -- not all ten. The table below shows the service categories and how many of the 329 listed records include each authorization in the ESMA CSV:

Custody is the most common service authorization in this table (219 records), followed by transfer services (202), exchange of crypto-assets for funds (181), and order execution (169). Operating a trading platform remains comparatively rare (21 records) -- reflecting the higher capital and technical requirements for multilateral trading venues under MiCA Article 68. Rows where ESMA provides description-only service text are mapped to the corresponding MiCA service category; rows with no service information are shown as unknown.

What is the MiCA transition deadline?

MiCA's transitional provisions (Article 143) allowed CASPs that were operating under national law before December 30, 2024 to continue providing services during a transition period. The maximum transition period was 18 months, ending July 1, 2026. As of this August 17, 2026 refresh, that maximum period has passed, and any CASP without MiCA authorization must cease operations in the EU unless a narrower national transitional position applies.

Transition deadline passed

The maximum MiCA transition period ended on July 1, 2026 for most member states. CASPs operating under national registrations can no longer rely on the maximum transitional window alone. If your exchange, custodian, or counterparty is not in this register, verify its current authorization position directly with the relevant NCA.

The 329 ESMA records still represent only firms and records that NCAs have reported into the interim register. The register can continue to change as authorizations are granted, amended, or withdrawn.

Why sanctions screening matters for every authorized CASP

MiCA authorization is necessary but not sufficient. Every authorized CASP must also comply with EU restrictive measures (sanctions), as implemented through Council regulations under the Common Foreign and Security Policy. The EBA Guidelines on restrictive measures include EBA/GL/2024/14 for institutions and EBA/GL/2024/15 for PSPs and CASPs performing transfers of funds and crypto-assets; they apply from December 30, 2025. Separately, EBA/GL/2024/01 amended the ML/TF Risk Factors Guidelines for CASPs and is not the restrictive-measures guidance.

The Chainalysis sanctions oracle -- the most widely used free on-chain screening tool -- checks addresses against OFAC, EU, and UN sanctions designations. It provides address-level lookups suitable for basic compliance checks. However, for CASPs subject to EBA restrictive-measures guidelines, the requirements go beyond address screening: you need documented procedures, match-handling workflows, audit trails proving each screening event, and the ability to demonstrate compliance to your NCA during supervisory reviews.

Frequently asked questions

How many CASPs are currently authorized under MiCA?

As of August 17, 2026, this table lists 329 ESMA CASP records across 26 home states. The latest record update in the official CSV is 2026-08-10. ESMA republishes the interim CSV regularly, so counterparties should still be checked against the current ESMA source file before onboarding.

What is the ESMA CASP register?

The ESMA CASP register is the official European Securities and Markets Authority database of all crypto-asset service providers authorized under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA). It is published as a downloadable CSV file on ESMA's website and updated regularly as national competent authorities (NCAs) across EEA member states grant new authorizations. The register includes entity names, LEI codes, authorized services, home member states, and authorization dates.

What services can MiCA-authorized CASPs provide?

MiCA defines ten categories of crypto-asset services in Article 3(1)(16): (a) custody and administration, (b) operation of a trading platform, (c) exchange of crypto-assets for funds, (d) exchange of crypto-assets for other crypto-assets, (e) execution of orders, (f) placing, (g) reception and transmission of orders, (h) advice, (i) portfolio management, and (j) transfer services. Each CASP is authorized for specific services listed in their authorization -- not all ten.

When is the MiCA transition deadline for existing CASPs?

The maximum MiCA transition period ended on July 1, 2026 for most member states. After this date, crypto-asset service providers operating in the EU must hold a full MiCA authorization or cease operations unless a narrower national transitional position applies. Some member states set shorter transition periods. CASPs that were registered under national regimes before MiCA took effect (December 30, 2024) could rely on transitional provisions only within the applicable national deadline and conditions.

Can a MiCA-authorized CASP operate across the entire EU?

Yes. MiCA authorization grants a single EU-wide passport. A CASP authorized by one national competent authority (NCA) can provide its authorized services across all 27 EU member states plus the EEA countries (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) without needing separate licenses in each country. The CASP must notify its home NCA before providing services in another member state, and the host NCA is informed, but no additional authorization is required.

Sanctions screening evidence for MiCA-authorized CASPs

MiCA-authorized CASPs should document list coverage, screening timing, list-version records, and match-resolution procedures before supervisory review.

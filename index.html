What to document

Not just a check. A record you can defend.

The screening itself is the easy part. The hard part is proving what was checked, when it was checked, and against which list version. The guides focus on that evidence layer first.

Before

Manual checks that collapse at audit time

Search one list at a time, copy screenshots, update spreadsheets, and hope someone can reconstruct the decision later. That works until a regulator, auditor, or bank asks for evidence.

After

A repeatable evidence workflow

Run the screening, retain the record, and keep something your team can actually use in review, audit prep, or partner due diligence. The useful outcome is a process your team can explain and defend.

Alternatives

You could also...

Use the free Chainalysis oracle
Covers US, EU, and UN lists -- but returns a binary yes/no with no audit trail, no risk scoring, no compliance certificates, no batch screening. When CySEC asks for timestamped screening records with list versions, you have nothing to show.
Pay for Chainalysis, Elliptic, or TRM Labs
$20K-$700K/year for bundled suites that include analytics, risk scoring, and transaction monitoring you may not need. If your obligation is sanctions screening + audit trail, you're paying 10-100x for features you won't use.
Screen manually using government websites
10-15 minutes per address across 3 separate websites. No systematic audit trail. No re-screening when lists update. No way to prove to CySEC what list version you checked against. Doesn't scale past 20 transactions/day.
Build your own screening system
Months of development. Ongoing list parsing, deduplication, update monitoring. You're not in the sanctions data business — and when OFAC changes their XML schema (they do), your parser breaks at the worst possible time.

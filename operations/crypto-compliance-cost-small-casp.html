How Much Does Crypto Compliance Cost for a Small EU CASP?

A sourced budget model for a small EU CASP: official regulator fees, public screening-tool pricing where available, and clearly labelled assumptions for staff, legal, audit, insurance, and training.

Updated July 10, 2026
9 min read
Sources: MiCA, EBA, CySEC, OpenSanctions

If you're building a compliance budget for a small EU-licensed crypto asset service provider, separate three things: official regulatory amounts, vendor prices that are public, and internal planning assumptions. Mixing those together is how old compliance-cost articles become false.

This refresh keeps only figures that are either from official regulator material, official vendor pricing pages, or explicitly marked as assumptions. It removes third-party pricing benchmarks, generic market averages, and enforcement statistics that were not suitable as inputs for a 2026 CASP budget.

The total depends on service scope, staff location, transaction volume, and whether screening is priced per query, per seat, or by enterprise quote. Do not use third-party "average contract value" pages as a compliance budget source unless the vendor confirms the quote in writing.

Planning model

A working first-year model for a 10-30 person CASP often lands around EUR 90,000 to 180,000 before prudential capital, but that is an assumption, not a market statistic. The only numbers you should treat as sourced inputs are official regulator fees, official vendor pricing, and signed quotes.

How does each cost category break down?

1. Staff: EUR 50,000 -- 120,000/yr

MiCA Article 68 requires CASPs to maintain governance arrangements, internal control mechanisms, and risk assessment mechanisms appropriate to their business. AML/CFT officer titles, seniority, and outsourcing limits are set by applicable AML rules and national implementation; the EU AML Regulation 2024/1624 becomes the main EU-level framework from 10 July 2027.

Planning salary assumptions to replace with local payroll data:

  • MLRO (Cyprus/Lithuania): EUR 45,000 -- 70,000/yr depending on experience
  • MLRO (Germany/Netherlands): EUR 70,000 -- 120,000/yr
  • Compliance officer (junior): EUR 35,000 -- 55,000/yr
  • Dual MLRO + compliance role: Assumption: EUR 55,000 -- 80,000/yr

For a firm with under 20 employees, budget at least one senior owner for AML/CFT, restrictive measures, regulatory correspondence, and evidence review. Whether that is a full-time employee, dual-role employee, or directly engaged specialist is a legal and supervisory question for the chosen jurisdiction.

2. Legal: EUR 15,000 -- 40,000/yr

You need legal counsel for three things: policy drafting, regulatory filings and correspondence with your NCA, and ongoing advisory as MiCA technical standards and national practices evolve. Treat the range below as a placeholder until you have written quotes.

For a first model, reserve EUR 15,000-40,000 per year for external legal support. The lower end assumes a limited advisory retainer and founder-owned documentation. The upper end assumes the firm actively handles filings, policy updates, and regulator correspondence.

3. Technology: official price, vendor quote, or volume model

Technology is where old benchmark articles become least reliable. Many crypto analytics and enterprise screening vendors use quote-only pricing. If a vendor does not publish an official price, use a written quote rather than a third-party pricing database.

Tool input Examples Budget treatment
Quote-only blockchain analytics Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic Use written vendor quote
Official per-query API OpenSanctions API EUR 0.10/query
Official list data + internal controls EU and national restrictive measures sources List access may be free; implementation is internal cost
Focused screening SaaS Any vendor with public pricing or quote Use official price page or quote
Example model 33,000 OpenSanctions API queries/year EUR 3,300 plus review and audit logging

OpenSanctions publishes a price of EUR 0.10 per API query. The EUR 3,300 example above is therefore a volume assumption: 33,000 queries per year. It does not include case review time, alert disposition, list-version evidence, storage, or engineering work.

For quote-only products, do not infer price from public deal databases. Ask the vendor to quote the exact service bundle, users, monitored wallets, blockchains, screening volume, data residency, audit-log export, and retention terms.

Where the biggest savings are

Technology is the line item with the widest quote spread, but cheaper is only acceptable if the control still works. Under the EBA restrictive-measures guidelines, CASPs should use a screening system adapted to their size, activity, and exposure. Screen against Union restrictive measures and relevant national restrictive measures; add OFAC, UK, UN-derived, or other lists when your legal footprint, counterparties, currencies, or risk assessment requires them.

4. Training: EUR 2,000 -- 10,000/yr

The EBA restrictive-measures guidelines require regular, role-appropriate training so staff remain aware of applicable restrictive measures, the firm's exposure assessment, and the firm's policies and controls. AML/CFT training obligations also depend on AML law and national implementation.

  • MLRO / compliance-owner certification: Assumption: EUR 1,500 -- 3,500 (replace with the provider's official course price)
  • Annual AML and restrictive-measures refreshers: Assumption: EUR 50 -- 200 per relevant staff member
  • MiCA-specific regulatory updates: Assumption: EUR 500 -- 2,000 for webinars, briefings, or counsel-led updates

For a 15-person company where 8 staff need role-specific awareness training, a EUR 3,000-5,000 placeholder is reasonable for planning. Do not cite it as a market average; replace it with current official course prices and internal time cost.

5. External audit: EUR 10,000 -- 30,000/yr

Do not assume a single EU-wide annual AML audit rule for every CASP category. Check the chosen NCA's authorisation conditions, national AML/CFT framework, board requirements, bank requirements, and auditor expectations. If an independent compliance review is required or useful, scope it separately from the financial audit.

Use EUR 10,000-30,000 only as a placeholder for a scoped AML/CFT, restrictive-measures, and controls review. Replace it with two written quotes that define sample size, systems tested, deliverables, remediation support, and whether the report is regulator-facing.

6. Insurance: EUR 500 -- 3,000/yr

Professional indemnity (PI) or errors and omissions (E&O) insurance may be relevant, but the required coverage depends on service category, contracts, bank expectations, and national supervision. Treat EUR 500-3,000 as a placeholder until a broker quotes the exact insured activity, territory, limit, deductible, and exclusions.

7. Regulatory fees: official NCA schedule only

Regulatory fees must come from the current NCA schedule. Cyprus is a useful example because CySEC publishes MiCAR CASP fee material. The simplified "EUR 5,000 annual renewal" wording is not accurate for all CySEC CASPs.

  • CySEC notification by a regulated financial entity under MiCA Article 60: EUR 10,000
  • CySEC CASP authorisation under MiCA Article 62: application fees are service-specific, including EUR 5,000 for exchange services, EUR 8,000 for execution/placing/reception-transmission/advice/portfolio-management services, EUR 10,000 for custody, and EUR 30,000 for operation of a trading platform
  • CySEC annual supervision fixed component: service-specific, including EUR 5,000, EUR 8,000, EUR 10,000, or EUR 20,000 annually depending on the MiCA service
  • CySEC annual supervision variable component: applies when crypto-asset-services turnover exceeds EUR 500,000, with bands of 1%, 0.4%, 0.3%, and 0.1% on the corresponding scale
  • Other member states: use the current official NCA fee schedule; this article does not retain unsourced country-by-country fee estimates

MiCA passporting

MiCA Article 65 provides a cross-border services process for authorised CASPs, but it is not a shortcut around controls. You still need to notify through the home competent authority, identify the services and member states, and maintain controls that fit the actual cross-border activity.

What can you save on — and what can you not?

Where you CAN reduce costs

  • Screening tools: Replace quote-only enterprise suites where a narrower tool still meets the risk assessment, list coverage, alert handling, audit trail, export, and retention requirements. See our full vendor comparison.
  • Training: Compare official prices for online and in-person training, then keep attendance, scope, and refresher evidence.
  • Legal: Ask for fixed-fee scopes for defined deliverables instead of open-ended hourly advice.
  • Jurisdiction: Choose jurisdiction using official fees, staffing reality, supervision fit, and service scope, not an old "cheapest country" table.

Where you CANNOT cut corners

  • Named ownership: Someone senior must own AML/CFT, restrictive-measures controls, and evidence. Outsourcing a tool or review does not outsource responsibility.
  • Screening controls: EBA GL/2024/15 expects CASPs performing crypto-asset transfers to use an adequate and reliable screening system for Union and relevant national restrictive measures.
  • Audit trail: Keep evidence of screened data, list source or version, alert review, decision, and action taken.
  • Independent review: If required by the NCA, board, bank, or authorisation conditions, scope and budget it explicitly.
  • Record retention: Set a statutory retention schedule with counsel and prove that records can be retrieved.
The cost of cutting corners on screening

MiCA Article 111 gives competent authorities administrative sanctions and measures for CASP infringements. For certain infringement categories, maximum administrative fines for legal persons include EUR 5,000,000 or 5% of total annual turnover, depending on the infringement and national implementation. Use the exact local transposition and supervisory position before relying on any penalty number.

Source: MiCA Regulation 2023/1114 Art. 111

What is the ROI of compliance vs. non-compliance?

The business case for compliance should not depend on invented fine averages. Compare your annual control budget against official penalty powers, remediation cost, business interruption, and licence risk.

Scenario Cost Outcome
Full compliance (affordable tools) Assumption: EUR 90,000 -- 150,000/yr Controls funded; replace with quotes
Full compliance (enterprise tools) Assumption: EUR 150,000+/yr Use written vendor quote
Minor compliance gap found Not estimated here Remediation, legal, and supervisory follow-up
Serious AML/sanctions failure MiCA maximums are infringement-specific Administrative measures, possible fine, suspension risk
License revocation Not estimated here Loss of authorisation basis

The practical takeaway is simple: unsupported savings are not real savings. A cheaper tool or staffing model only helps if it still produces evidence the NCA, bank, auditor, and board can understand.

Licence withdrawal is the worst operational outcome. If the home-state authorisation is withdrawn, the CASP loses the basis for the cross-border services it notified under MiCA.

Bottom line

There is no official EU-wide average compliance budget for a small CASP. A defensible budget separates official fees, official vendor prices, signed quotes, and assumptions. For many small firms, the working model will be around EUR 90,000-180,000 before prudential capital, but that number must be rebuilt from your own facts.

The non-negotiables are named ownership, screening controls for Union and relevant national restrictive measures, evidence of alert handling, and records that can be retrieved. OFAC, UK, UN-derived, and other lists may also be required, but that depends on legal nexus and exposure rather than a blanket EU mandate.

The question is not whether a tool is cheap. It is whether the control is adequate, reliable, documented, and proportionate to the CASP's actual business. That is what the budget has to buy.

Cut screening waste without cutting coverage

Small CASPs can often avoid enterprise-suite pricing by choosing focused tools and documenting exactly how list coverage, timestamps, exports, and audit evidence work.

Frequently asked questions

How much does crypto compliance cost for a small EU CASP?

There is no official EU-wide average. This article uses a planning model: official regulator fees and public vendor prices are sourced, while staff, legal, audit, training, and insurance numbers are labelled assumptions to replace with quotes and payroll data.

What is the cheapest EU country to get a CASP license?

Do not rely on an unsourced cheapest-country ranking. Compare current official NCA fee schedules, required services, capital category, local staffing costs, and supervisory expectations. For Cyprus, CySEC publishes MiCAR CASP application and annual supervision fees by service type.

Do I need a full-time MLRO for a small crypto company?

MiCA requires CASPs to maintain governance and internal control arrangements. AML/CFT officer titles, seniority, and outsourcing limits depend on applicable AML rules and national implementation. For budgeting, assume a named senior AML/compliance owner; outsourcing a control does not outsource the CASP's responsibility.

How much do sanctions screening tools cost for crypto companies?

Use official vendor pricing where it exists. OpenSanctions publishes EUR 0.10 per API query. Many blockchain analytics and enterprise screening vendors publish quote-only pricing, so their costs should be budgeted from written quotes rather than third-party pricing benchmarks. What happens if a CASP fails a compliance inspection?

MiCA Article 111 gives competent authorities administrative sanctions and measures. For certain infringements by legal persons, maximum administrative fines include EUR 5,000,000 or 5% of total annual turnover, depending on the infringement category and national implementation. Regulators can also impose supervisory measures, including suspension or withdrawal of authorisation.

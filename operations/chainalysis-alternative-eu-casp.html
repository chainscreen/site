Why do CASPs look for Chainalysis alternatives?

Chainalysis publishes a broad compliance and investigations stack: KYT for continuous transaction monitoring, Address Screening for wallet and liquidity-pool risk, VASP Risking for counterparty due diligence, and Reactor for investigations. Its Reactor page says the product traces activity across 27+ blockchains and 40M+ assets.

The practical question is whether a 5-person CASP in Limassol processing 30 transactions per day needs the same tooling footprint as an exchange, bank, or investigations team. For many small EU-licensed crypto companies, the answer is no.

Here is the pricing reality from official pages reviewed on 2026-07-10:

  • Vendor-published paid pricing: Chainalysis does not publish a standard paid CASP price on its product pages.
  • Scope: Paid Chainalysis tools cover monitoring, screening, risk settings, continuous alerts, investigation workflows, and counterparty risk. The quote depends on the modules, volume, users, and deployment model.
  • Practical step: Request a written quote for the exact modules, users, chains, and transaction volume you need.

For a small CASP, the cost question should start with scope: do you need transaction monitoring, investigation tooling, entity attribution, Travel Rule workflows, sanctions screening, or only a documented screening control with alert resolution?

The core question

Do you need a full blockchain analytics suite, or do you need controls that satisfy EBA GL/2024/15 for applicable Union and national restrictive measures? For many small CASPs, the answer is a narrower screening and evidence workflow.

What do you actually need vs. what are you paying for?

Chainalysis bundles several products into their compliance platform. Understanding what each does -- and which ones your regulator actually inspects -- helps separate must-haves from nice-to-haves.

What EBA and CySEC expect you to evidence

Based on EBA Guidelines GL/2024/15 (applicable from 30 December 2025) and CySEC Circular C685, regulators look for evidence that your screening controls work:

The 1 July 2026 MiCA date is the maximum transitional period for certain providers that were already offering crypto-asset services under national law before 30 December 2024. It is not a MiCA-wide date that makes OFAC, UK OFSI, or UN screening mandatory for every EU CASP.

  1. Applicable restrictive measures -- EBA GL/2024/15 covers Union restrictive measures and relevant national restrictive measures. CySEC C685 says its inspections focused on UN and EU lists and relevant U.S. and UK screening practices.
  2. Pre-transfer screening -- CASPs should screen crypto-asset transfers before making crypto-assets available to the beneficiary.
  3. Alert documentation -- Can you show how alerts were investigated, who decided true/false positives, and what action was taken?
  4. Ongoing screening and list updates -- Do you re-screen customer databases when relevant lists change and test list-management accuracy?
  5. Documented procedures -- Do you have written policies for alert handling, escalation, freezing or blocking, reporting, testing, and vendor oversight?

What Chainalysis bundles that you may not need

  • KYT (Know Your Transaction): Continuous transaction monitoring with real-time alerts. Useful where transaction volume and risk justify automated monitoring.
  • Reactor: Investigation tooling for tracing funds across wallets, assets, swaps, bridges, DEXs, and mixers.
  • Address Screening and VASP Risking: Wallet, liquidity-pool, and counterparty-risk tools that may be useful if your risk assessment requires them.

If your primary control need is sanctions screening with documented alert handling, do not buy broader analytics or investigations modules unless your risk assessment supports them.

Decision framework: which alternative fits your CASP?

The right tool depends on what you actually need, not what sounds impressive in a sales demo. Here's a framework based on operational reality:

You need full blockchain analytics + investigations

Stay with Chainalysis, or evaluate TRM Labs and Elliptic.

  • If your compliance team runs investigations, traces funds across wallets, and produces intelligence reports, you need the full suite
  • Validate chain coverage, investigation workflows, deployment model, and case evidence against your risk assessment
  • Budget: quote-based; official product pages reviewed do not publish standard annual prices
You need transaction monitoring (KYT) + screening

Look at Scorechain, Crystal Intelligence, or Merkle Science.

  • If your volume and risk assessment require continuous monitoring with risk scores, compare the monitoring scope before buying investigation tooling
  • Check whether the vendor publishes audit exports, alert-resolution records, testing support, and list-update evidence
  • Budget: quote-based unless the vendor gives you a written fixed package
You need sanctions screening only + audit trail

Evaluate focused screening vendors and free APIs, then add the missing evidence workflow.

  • If your control need is sanctions list checks with proper documentation, verify the tool covers applicable Union and national restrictive measures
  • Confirm exports, alert-resolution records, list-update handling, durable audit records, and testing support before relying on it
  • Use free APIs only as an input to a documented process, not as the entire compliance control
  • Budget: depends on whether you build the records and testing layer yourself or buy it from a vendor
You only need basic screening (no audit trail requirement)

The Chainalysis free oracle/API or Scorechain free sanctions API may be sufficient.

  • If you're not regulated and only need a basic sanctions check, Chainalysis says its oracle includes sanctions designations from organizations including the US, EU, or UN; Scorechain says its free API checks supported sanctions lists across 21+ blockchains
  • Build your own records, testing, and alert handling if you later need regulated evidence
  • Budget: $0 + engineering time for audit trail

What is the real cost of Chainalysis's "free" oracle?

Chainalysis offers a free sanctions screening API and an on-chain oracle. The oracle documentation says it checks whether a wallet address has been included in a sanctions designation and is updated for sanctions designations from organizations including the US, EU, or UN. We've written a detailed technical analysis of what the free oracle covers and what it doesn't.

The short version: the oracle returns a boolean result. Here's what that means for an EU CASP:

Gap analysis: Chainalysis free vs. EU requirements

Covered by the oracle docs: Address checks against sanctions designations from organizations including the US, EU, or UN.

Not stated in the oracle docs reviewed: UK OFSI coverage, case management, alert-resolution workflow, or regulator-ready exports.

Not provided by a boolean oracle call: documented alert analysis, screening-system testing, list-update evidence, freezing/blocking procedures, or reporting procedures.

Bottom line: The free oracle can be a useful screening input. A regulated CASP still needs the control framework around it.

This doesn't mean you should stop using the free tools. Use them as a screening layer. But you need additional infrastructure for records, alert handling, testing, and regulator-ready documentation -- either build it yourself or use a purpose-built tool.

Chainalysis's own documentation includes a standard disclaimer: "Chainalysis cannot guarantee the accuracy, timeliness, suitability, or validity of the data." That's reasonable for a free product. But it's a weak foundation for your entire compliance program.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Chainalysis so expensive?

Chainalysis does not publish standard paid pricing on its official product pages. Its paid stack includes KYT, Address Screening, Reactor, VASP Risking, APIs, monitoring, and investigation workflows, so small CASPs should request a scoped quote instead of assuming they need the full platform.

Is the Chainalysis free sanctions oracle enough for EU compliance?

Not on its own for a regulated CASP. Chainalysis says its free oracle checks whether an address is included in sanctions designations from organizations including the US, EU, or UN, and it returns a boolean result. EBA GL/2024/15 expects policies, alert analysis, documented decisions, screening-system testing, and freezing/reporting procedures for applicable restrictive measures. See our full analysis of the free oracle's limitations.

Can a small CASP pass a CySEC inspection without Chainalysis?

Yes, if its controls are effective. EBA GL/2024/15 and CySEC C685 do not name a required vendor. CySEC emphasizes configuration, list updates, testing, alert handling, ongoing screening, and documented procedures, so the evidence matters more than the vendor label.

What sanctions lists must an EU CASP screen against?

EBA GL/2024/15 requires controls for Union restrictive measures and relevant national restrictive measures; it does not make OFAC or UK OFSI screening mandatory for every EU CASP. CySEC C685 says its inspections focused on UN and EU sanctions lists and also reviewed relevant U.S. and UK screening practices. CASPs should document which lists apply to their footprint and why.

How do I switch from Chainalysis to a cheaper alternative without compliance gaps?

Run both tools in parallel for a defined transition period. Screen the same addresses and transfers through both systems, compare alerts, verify coverage for applicable EU and national restrictive measures, test list-update handling, export alert-resolution records, and have the compliance owner approve the change before decommissioning the old tool.

Bottom line

Chainalysis publishes a broad product stack for monitoring, screening, risk assessment, and investigations. If your actual control need is sanctions screening with documented alert handling -- not full-suite blockchain analytics -- evaluate narrower tools before buying a broader platform.

The decision framework is straightforward:

  • Need analytics + investigations? Stay with Chainalysis, TRM, or Elliptic. You're paying for real capabilities you'll use.
  • Need KYT + screening? Evaluate Scorechain, Crystal Intelligence, Merkle Science, and similar platforms against your transaction volume, chains, and evidence needs.
  • Need Travel Rule operations? Evaluate 21 Analytics alongside a sanctions-screening or wallet-risk tool.
  • Need screening only? Evaluate focused screening vendors and free APIs, but build or buy the audit, testing, and alert-resolution layer.

Whatever you choose, make sure it covers applicable Union and national restrictive measures, documents why any UN, U.S., or UK screening is included for your footprint, preserves alert-resolution evidence, and triggers re-screening when relevant lists update. The vendor name on the invoice does not replace the control evidence.

If you run a small or mid-sized Crypto-Asset Service Provider in the EU, you already know you need sanctions screening. What's less clear is which tool to use and how much to pay. Enterprise vendors don't publish prices. Free tools have silent gaps. And the compliance cost of choosing wrong can be material.

1. What are the three pricing tiers in the market?

For buying decisions, it is useful to group the sanctions screening market for crypto companies into three tiers. The gap between official data sources, focused screening tools, and enterprise blockchain analytics suites is significant.

Tier Annual Cost Vendors Best For
Enterprise Quote required Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic Exchanges, custodians, banks with crypto exposure
Mid-market Published/estimated $6K+ Scorechain, Crystal Intelligence, Merkle Science Licensed CASPs with moderate volume
Budget Free, usage-based, or quote sanctions.io, OpenSanctions, AMLBot Small CASPs, startups, compliance-first ops

Enterprise tools bundle sanctions screening with transaction monitoring (KYT), risk scoring, investigation tools, and case management. If you only need a narrow sanctions-list control, you may be paying for features you do not use; if you are an EU PSP or CASP, EBA GL/2024/15 still expects the surrounding procedures, alert handling, list-update controls, and system testing to be documented.

4. What are the free screening options and their limitations?

Several free options exist. Each has real value but also significant gaps that compliance teams should understand before relying on them.

Chainalysis Free Sanctions API & Oracle

  • What it does: Screens blockchain addresses against sanctions designations from economic/trade embargo lists including the US, EU, or UN. REST API access is advertised separately; the on-chain oracle is available for smart contract integration.
  • What it doesn't do: No retained evidence package. No risk scoring. No historical screening records. No batch processing. Binary yes/no result only.
  • Verdict: Useful as a free address check. But a binary result without documented alert handling, list-update controls, and retained evidence is not enough on its own for an EU-regulated CASP's restrictive-measures control framework.

OFAC SDN List (direct download)

  • What it does: The official OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list, downloadable in XML, CSV, and other formats. Free. Updated frequently (multiple times per week).
  • What it doesn't do: Official SDN/SLS data and search access only; no wallet-to-entity analytics, case management, retained audit trail, or alert workflow. Digital currency address searches in OFAC's Sanctions List Search use the ID # field and return exact matches only.
  • Verdict: A data source, not a screening tool. Requires significant engineering to use effectively.

EU Consolidated Sanctions List (direct download)

  • What it does: The official EU restrictive measures list. XML/CSV downloads from the European Commission. Free. Updated when new measures are adopted.
  • What it doesn't do: Same limitations as OFAC direct download. No blockchain-specific identifiers (addresses are rarely listed directly — entities are).
  • Verdict: Essential data source for EU compliance, but requires entity-to-address resolution that only specialized tools provide.
The free tier gap

Free tools can cover multiple sanctions lists, but list coverage is only part of the control. EBA GL/2024/15 applies from 30 December 2025 and expects PSPs and CASPs to choose an adequate screening system, specify applicable restrictive measures, update screening data when measures change, document alert decisions, understand system limitations, and test the system regularly. Free tools rarely provide that full operating framework.

5. What is the real cost of manual screening?

Some small CASPs still screen manually — a compliance officer checks addresses against downloaded lists, searches OFAC's website, and records results in a spreadsheet. This feels free. It is not.

Volume Time per Day Annual Labor Cost Equivalent Tool Cost
10 checks/day 20–45 min $4,000–9,000 Most tools are comparable
50 checks/day 1.5–4 hours $20,000–50,000 Tools are cheaper at this point
100 checks/day 3–8 hours $40,000–100,000 Even enterprise tools are cheaper

Assumptions: Compliance officer salary EUR 35–55K/yr in Cyprus, 2–5 minutes per manual check including documentation, 250 working days/year.

Beyond the labor cost, manual screening carries hidden risks:

  • Human error: Typos, missed matches, inconsistent name matching. An automated tool checks the same way every time.
  • No real-time updates: If OFAC adds an address at 2pm and your team downloads lists weekly, you have a gap.
  • Weak audit trail: A spreadsheet is a weak audit trail unless access, changes, timestamps, and evidence attachments are controlled. When an NCA asks "show me the screening record for transaction X on date Y," a manual process can struggle to answer quickly.
  • Opportunity cost: Your compliance officer should be building policies and managing risk — not copy-pasting addresses into search boxes.

The break-even point is roughly 50 checks per day. Below that, manual screening is defensible (if well-documented). Above that, tools are both cheaper and more reliable.

6. What is the cost of non-compliance?

For context on why this matters, here is what inadequate sanctions compliance can cost a crypto business:

No reliable MiCA fine average

There is no reliable public "average MiCA sanctions fine" for 2024, and MiCA does not make OFAC, UK, UN, and EU list coverage universally mandatory for every EU CASP. The relevant baseline is narrower and more fact-specific: applicable Union and national restrictive measures, plus any additional regimes that apply because of your jurisdiction, customers, counterparties, banking partners, or risk appetite.

Sanctions failures can still trigger blocked assets, delayed withdrawals, customer harm, reporting obligations, supervisory findings, remediation costs, and in some jurisdictions civil or criminal enforcement. The right comparison is not just tool price versus fine size; it is tool price versus the cost of operating and evidencing a defensible control manually.

More practically: an EU national competent authority can ask how you chose the screening system, which restrictive measures you apply, when lists are updated, how alerts are investigated, and where decisions are recorded. A tool that exports evidence is useful only if your written procedure explains how that evidence is produced, reviewed, and retained.

7. Which tool is recommended for your budget?

Budget < EUR 5,000/yr

Recommended stack: official list data and/or a free wallet oracle + your own evidence and alert workflow.

  • Use official EU/OFAC list data, OpenSanctions API access, and/or a free address oracle as inputs
  • You'll need to build your own evidence store, alert workflow, list-update process, and compliance reporting
  • Gap: No KYT (transaction monitoring). No unified evidence trail. Significant engineering and compliance effort to build the operating framework EBA expects.
  • This works for very small operations (<20 checks/day) with engineering capacity to build the compliance infrastructure around free or low-cost inputs
Mid-range EUR 6,000–15,000/yr

Recommended: Scorechain for an all-in-one platform, or a focused screening vendor if sanctions screening is the only immediate gap.

  • Scorechain has public third-party pricing at EUR 6K/yr; verify included modules, users, API limits, and support before treating that as total cost
  • Focused screening vendors can be cheaper than full KYT platforms, but you must verify audit trail, list-update controls, and data-processing commitments before relying on them
  • Confirm EU and applicable national restrictive-measures coverage first; add OFAC, UK, UN, and other lists where your exposure assessment or counterparties require them
  • This tier suits licensed CASPs with 50–500 checks/day
Enterprise Direct quote

Recommended: Chainalysis, Elliptic, or TRM Labs — depending on your chain coverage needs and procurement preferences.

  • Chainalysis for a mature KYT and investigation suite
  • Elliptic for 60+ chain coverage, Holistic Screening, and cross-chain investigation workflows
  • TRM Labs for broad wallet-screening coverage and investigation capabilities
  • Crystal Intelligence if you need 330+ chain coverage and should verify quote, depth of attribution, and audit exports for your specific chains
  • At this tier, you get full KYT, investigation tools, case management, and dedicated support
